# Lietuva Chat Should Own Lithuania's Cross-Agency Gap

Lietuva Chat should build an **owned, versioned index of official Lithuanian sources in PostgreSQL (pgvector plus Lithuanian full-text search), with Exa restricted to an allowlist of government domains as a fallback for gaps and volatile facts**. It should start with the three machine-readable feeds that already exist (TAR legal acts with consolidated versions, the JAR company register and the VMI/Sodra open datasets on data.gov.lt) and then crawl, politely, the HTML guidance that residents and foreigners actually need: migracija.lt, vmi.lt, sodra.lt, the VLK, epaslaugos.lt, the URM diaspora portals and, last, the 60 municipal sites. The position is open. Lithuania has **no national generative citizen assistant**, only single-agency, pre-LLM bots such as VMI's "Simas" and viLTė, which work in Lithuanian or Lithuanian plus English. America.gov, launched on 29 September 2026, has made "AI front door to government" a mainstream idea and has already been criticised as "solving the easiest 5% of the problem." Lietuva Chat should not imitate a state portal. It should be the independent, multilingual (LT/EN/RU/UK), cross-agency assistant for foreigners, relocators and the diaspora, with every claim cited and dated. The legal constraints are workable but strict. Official legal texts are free of copyright. Ministry explainer pages are not clearly free and fall under the opt-out-able commercial text-and-data-mining (TDM) exception. Article 50(1) of the AI Act (disclosure that the user is talking to an AI) has applied since 2 August 2026. Lithuanian personal codes need special handling under national law. Using "Lietuva" alongside state heraldry or gov-like styling invites impersonation and misleading-practice claims.

## America.gov proves the format and exposes the weakness Lietuva Chat should exploit

America.gov is the Trump administration's AI search-and-referral chatbot, launched on **29 September 2026** with an executive order. It is pitched as "one front door for every single question" across about **29,000 federal websites** ([FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/trump-launches-ai-site-america-gov/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/can-a-chatbot-fix-the-government-maze-the-white-house-is-about-to-find-out/)). Google confirmed that Gemini powers it, and Joe Gebbia said xAI's Grok was also used ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/can-a-chatbot-fix-the-government-maze-the-white-house-is-about-to-find-out/)). That is essentially the same stack as Lietuva Chat (a frontier model grounded in official sites), so technology will not differentiate the two. It cannot carry out transactions. Medicare enrollment, passport applications and job applications are "planned features, not available functions," and transactions and status tracking are slated for 2027 ([Yahoo/News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/u-government-just-launched-ai-154507187.html); [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/trump-launches-ai-site-america-gov/)). The early criticism lays out a playbook for Lietuva Chat. Former USDS head Mikey Dickerson called it "solving the easiest 5% of the problem" ([FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/trump-launches-ai-site-america-gov/)). NBC found it often links to a Clinton-era site and that its answers sometimes say less than the sources they cite ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/trump-america-gov-ai-chatbot-often-links-clinton-era-website-rcna600540)). TechCrunch flagged hallucination risk on visas, taxes and benefits, where a wrong answer means a missed deadline ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/can-a-chatbot-fix-the-government-maze-the-white-house-is-about-to-find-out/)). Its privacy stance is now the baseline any rival has to match: **no account, queries cached as a hash, responses cached for up to two hours, and a notice that "AI providers do not retain your prompts or responses"** ([FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/trump-launches-ai-site-america-gov/)).

Other governments split into three models. The first is information-only retrieval over official content: GOV.UK Chat, which works only in the app behind One Login, covers about 80,000 pages and gives no legal advice ([GDS](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2026/05/14/gov-uk-chat-launches/)); Poland's mObywatel assistant, which is anonymous, refuses off-topic questions and runs on the Polish PLLuM model ([ITwiz](https://itwiz.pl/asystent-ai-dostepny-w-mobywatelu/)); and America.gov. The second is transactional agents wired into state registers: Ukraine's Diia.AI, which checks eligibility and issues documents ([GovInsider](https://govinsider.asia/intl-en/article/ukraine-launches-worlds-first-government-ai-agent)), and Abu Dhabi's TAMM, which covers 940+ services ([ITU WSIS](https://www.itu.int/net4/wsis/stocktaking/Prizes/Prizes/Details/17391993737703194)). The third is a federated network of agency bots, Estonia's Bürokratt ([e-Estonia](https://e-estonia.com/estonias-new-virtual-assistant-aims-to-rewrite-the-way-people-interact-with-public-services/)). An independent service cannot reach state registers, so the transactional model is out. Every state model is also confined to one country and one government's content, which leaves cross-border and cross-agency questions unserved.

Lithuania itself has no assistant in any of these categories. Its bots are scattered and pre-LLM. viLTė answered COVID-era questions in LT and EN ([EIM](https://eimin.lrv.lt/en/sector-activities/digital-policy/artificial-intelligence/)). VMI's Simas, built by Tilde, has run since December 2021 and handled **94,000+ inquiries in its first five months**, in Lithuanian only ([Tilde](https://tilde.ai/case-study/artificial-intelligence-programs-used-to-consult-the-public-in-lithuania/)). Support for foreigners is mostly human: International House Vilnius reports **49,000+ clients** and runs no chatbot ([InterregEurope](https://www.interregeurope.eu/good-practices/international-house-vilnius-one-stop-service-centre-for-international-talents)). The National AI Strategic Guidelines 2026–2035 promise "a more active role for the state" in public-service AI but no specific citizen assistant ([EU Digital Skills & Jobs](https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/initiatives/national-strategies/lithuania-national-artificial-intelligence-strategic-guidelines)). Open Lithuanian LLMs released in 2026 would let the state build one quickly ([VSSA](https://vssa.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/sukurtas-pirmasis-lietuviu-kalbos-dirbtinio-intelekto-modelis-lietuviu-tyreju-zingsnis-i-di-ateiti-yx9/)). A 2024 Seimas resolution requires a human in the loop for any state AI decision affecting citizens' rights ([infoerdve.lt](https://infoerdve.lt/en/lithuania-outlines-vision-for-ai-driven-digital-governance)), so a future state assistant would most likely stay informational, which is the same lane as Lietuva Chat.

That points to one positioning. A generic Lithuanian-language assistant for citizens is the use case most exposed to a free state product. The defensible niches are the ones no state system targets. The first is **foreigners and relocators**, who need answers in RU and UK as well as EN. The second is **the diaspora**: citizenship, voting abroad, pensions across EU states, and return. The third is **questions that span VMI, Sodra, the Migration Department, Užimtumo tarnyba and a municipality in a single answer**. The answer to America.gov is depth rather than a bigger index: answer the question in full, quote the source, show when it was last checked, and route to the right official channel for anything case-specific. The service must also say plainly that it is not the government. America.gov draws its credibility from the .gov domain, and Lietuva Chat must not borrow that credibility.

## Three machine-readable feeds exist; the guidance users need is HTML behind bot walls

Lithuanian government information comes in three tiers, and the ingestion plan should follow them.

**Tier 1 is open data with APIs.** data.gov.lt, run by VSSA, serves datasets through **get.data.gov.lt** using the national UAPI standard on the open-source Spinta engine. Reads need no authentication. The API supports filter, select, sort and cursor pagination, plus a **`/:changes` incremental feed** that returns insert, patch and delete operations ([docs.data.gov.lt](https://docs.data.gov.lt/projects/atviriduomenys/latest/api/index.html)). Spinta published **904 datasets from 177 organisations** in 2025 ([data.gov.lt blog](https://data.gov.lt/blog/)). The most valuable dataset for a services chatbot is the **TAR legal-acts register, with separate models for documents and consolidated versions** ("suvestinės redakcijos"), which the Seimas Chancellery and the State Data Agency opened together ([VDA](https://vda.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/valstybes-duomenu-agentura-ir-seimo-kanceliarija-teises-aktu-duomenis-atvere-inovacijoms-dabar-pasiekiami-atviru-duomenu-formatu/); [data.gov.lt/datasets/2613](https://data.gov.lt/datasets/2613/)). A separate TAR integration API exists, but it requires a data-access agreement ([lrs.lt DOK-6.1](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=32584)). Business data is also open. The JAR company register on data.gov.lt is updated daily ([data.gov.lt/datasets/1484](https://data.gov.lt/datasets/1484/)). VMI's taxes-paid-by-legal-entities dataset is daily and **CC BY 4.0** ([data.gov.lt/datasets/673](https://data.gov.lt/datasets/673/)). Sodra publishes monthly employer-level JSON and CSV, plus a SOAP service, at atvira.sodra.lt ([atvira.sodra.lt](https://atvira.sodra.lt/imones/rinkiniai/index.html)). These feeds answer "what does the law say" and "is company X a VAT payer." They do not answer "how do I renew my residence permit."

**Tier 2 is HTML guidance, which is what users actually ask about.** The citizen entry point is the EVV portal at **epaslaugos.lt**, run by VSSA. It began its **largest modernisation in 15 years in mid-August 2026**, with a new platform and a rebuilt service catalogue ([15min.lt](https://www.15min.lt/verslas/naujiena/mokslas-it/elektroniniai-valdzios-vartai-atsinaujina-gyventoju-laukia-svarbus-pokyciai-1290-2745150)), so old `/portal/content/NNNNN` URLs may already be stale. Ministries and agencies share the **`<institution>.lrv.lt`** template under VSSA's common website requirements ([vssa.lrv.lt](https://vssa.lrv.lt/lt/veiklos-sritys/instituciju-interneto-svetaines/)). One extractor therefore covers most of them. Migration has two domains. **migracija.lt** is the MIGRIS portal, with EN service guides such as the temporary-residence-permit page ([migracija.lt](https://www.migracija.lt/en/noriu-gauti-pakeisti-leidima-laikinai-gyventi)). **migracija.lrv.lt** carries fees, forms and news. For example, the e-resident status fee page lists **€90 for 3 years** ([migracija.lrv.lt](https://migracija.lrv.lt/lt/paslaugos/valstybes-rinkliavu-sarasas/e-rezidento-statusas/)). Health insurance guidance sits on ligoniukasa.lrv.lt and e.vlk.lt ([e.vlk.lt](https://e.vlk.lt/)). Diaspora content has moved under the Ministry of Foreign Affairs: **globalilietuva.urm.lt** with the "Grįžtu LT" return centre, and keliauk.urm.lt for citizenship and consular matters ([globalilietuva.urm.lt](https://globalilietuva.urm.lt/); [keliauk.urm.lt](https://keliauk.urm.lt/gyvenantiems-uzsienyje/konsulines-funkcijos-lietuvos-pilieciams/lietuvos-respublikos-pilietybe)). The older "Renkuosi Lietuvą" centre is defunct and must be excluded ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/lituanica/norintiems-sugrizti/754/2161674/uzsienio-reikalu-ministerija-pradeda-teikti-informacija-grizimo-i-lietuva-klausimais)). The operational problem is that **migracija.lrv.lt, vmi.lt and data.gov.lt pages returned HTTP 403 to an automated fetcher during the research session**. Bot protection is a real constraint, not a hypothetical one.

**Tier 3 is personal lookups that the bot should link to and never call.** Examples are the PSD insurance-status check at dpsdr.vlk.lt, which asks for date of birth and part of the personal code ([vlmedicina.lt](https://www.vlmedicina.lt/lt/vlk-internetu-galima-suzinoti-ar-esate-draustas-sveikatos-draudimu)), VMI i.MAS web services, which need a subscription ([vmi.lt](https://www.vmi.lt/evmi/bendroji-informacija-apie-i.mas)), and MIGRIS application status. Logging in to these services uses an ID card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID, e-banking or a qualified e-signature ([agrello.io](https://www.agrello.io/en/blog/cross-border-digital-identity-and-e-signing-in-the-baltic-states-a-practical-guide-for-smes/)). Lietuva Chat should explain these methods and **never ask users for eID or bank-link credentials**, because that would look like phishing.

Municipalities are the hardest layer. No shared municipal content platform was found. Each of the **60 municipalities** runs its own site, often from a private vendor ([tobalt.lt](https://tobalt.lt/svetaines-savivaldybems-2026/)). The mandatory VSSA content structure (with **96% municipal compliance**) makes the required sections scrapable in a predictable way ([vssa.lrv.lt](https://vssa.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/ivertintas-valstybes-ir-savivaldybiu-instituciju-ir-istaigu-interneto-svetainiu-atitikimas-bendriesiems-reikalavimams-tom/)). The 2026 amendment to the reuse law, **XV-564, in force from 1 January 2026**, extends data-opening duties to municipal institutions ([e-seimas XV-564](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/portal/legalAct/lt/TAD/d9c2b5a2c61311f08962cdb35f3f1615)), so more municipal data should appear on data.gov.lt over time.

## A Postgres-native hybrid index beats live search on control, citation and evaluation

The production precedents keep their own index. GOV.UK Chat retrieves chunks of GOV.UK content from OpenSearch, uses "hierarchical semantic chunking ... preserving the HTML header structure," and re-ranks on metadata designed together with content designers ([Inside GOV.UK](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2026/05/15/developing-gov-uk-chat-our-data-science-and-ai-engineering-journey/); [GOV.UK Chat ADR 0001](https://docs.publishing.service.gov.uk/repos/govuk-chat/adr/0001-pilot-technical-architecture.html)). Estonia's Bürokratt scrapes and cleans pages that agencies submit into a Common Knowledge Base, and its code is MIT-licensed and reusable ([OSOR](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/open-source-observatory-osor/document/digital-public-services-based-open-source-case-study-burokratt)). Cost is not the argument for owning the index. Exa search runs at roughly **$7 per 1,000 requests**, according to third-party pricing pages ([tinyfish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/exa-pricing)), which is small next to LLM tokens. The argument is **control over scope, article-level legal citation, version history, and the ability to evaluate retrieval**. Exa's coverage of small `.lt` agency subdomains is untested, and its ranking cannot be tuned.

The recommended design keeps the current LangGraph and Exa stack and promotes PostgreSQL from optional to required. The primary leg is pgvector plus Postgres full-text search over the crawled allowlist, fused with reciprocal rank fusion. The secondary leg is Exa with `includeDomains` set to the same allowlist, triggered when owned-retrieval scores fall below a threshold or the query is time-sensitive. A third, optional step re-fetches the one or two cited pages at answer time with `maxAgeHours: 0`. Exa documents this setting as the way to force freshness for prices and similar volatile pages, together with a 12–15 s `livecrawlTimeout` for slow sites ([Exa best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/contents-best-practices); [Exa Contents](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/get-contents)).

**Crawling** starts from sitemaps and uses an identifying User-Agent with a contact address, one or two concurrent requests per host, and a delay of at least one second. The crawler honours robots.txt and TDM-reservation signals and logs the robots.txt state at fetch time as evidence. HTML is converted with trafilatura or self-hosted Crawl4AI, and PDFs go through a separate extractor. A competitor-published benchmark puts Crawl4AI's anti-bot success at 72% against Firecrawl's 88% ([spider.cloud, biased](https://spider.cloud/blog/firecrawl-vs-crawl4ai-vs-spider-honest-benchmark/)). The 403s observed on lrv.lt and vmi.lt matter more here, though. The right answer to them is asking VSSA and the agencies for access or an allowlisted crawler, not escalating anti-bot evasion. **Change detection** stores the ETag, Last-Modified and a SHA-256 of normalised text for each URL. It sends conditional GETs and re-chunks and re-embeds only when the hash changes, because many CMS pages send no validators.

**Legal texts** need their own schema. Each TAR consolidated version is stored as a versioned document and chunked on legal structure (straipsnis → dalis → punktas), with `act_id, version_id, valid_from, valid_to, article_no, heading_path, source_url` and a breadcrumb prefix added before embedding. Retrieval then defaults to the version in force today and can still answer "what was the rule in 2024." TAR deep links follow stable patterns such as `e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalAct/<id>/asr` ([e-tar](https://www.e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalAct/TAR.FA13E28615F6/asr)).

**Lithuanian retrieval** has no public benchmark, so the team must build its own. Lithuanian has seven grammatical cases, which makes unstemmed keyword search weak. PostgreSQL's source tree ships a Snowball Lithuanian stemmer ([postgres/postgres](https://github.com/postgres/postgres/tree/master/src/backend/snowball/libstemmer)), but the docs do not list it, so confirm it on the deployed version with `SELECT to_tsvector('lithuanian','mokesčių')`. Index both with and without diacritics (`unaccent`), since users type "zemes mokestis." Shortlist **BGE-M3**, which is open, offers dense and sparse retrieval, and scores nDCG@10 of 70.0 on MIRACL ([aimultiple](https://aimultiple.com/open-source-embedding-models)), against a commercial multilingual embedder. Then pick by Recall@10 on an in-house LT/EN/RU query set. The state's LT-MLKM-modernBERT is a masked language model, not a ready-made embedder ([HF](https://huggingface.co/VSSA-SDSA/LT-MLKM-modernBERT)). For generation, keep Grok or GPT, but expect weak spots: frontier models' accuracy on Lithuanian case marking ranged from **0.662 to 0.852** ([LoResLM 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.loreslm-1.32/)). Prompt the model to quote source wording for tax and legal terms rather than paraphrase them.

**Scale is modest.** Assume 100,000 pages at about 1,500 tokens each. That is about 300,000 chunks and roughly 1.8 GB of raw 1536-dimension vectors, which one Postgres instance with an HNSW index handles without a separate vector database. Embedding the whole corpus once costs single-digit to low-double-digit dollars with OpenAI's small or large models; those prices were not verified this session. A self-hosted crawl at one request per second per host across about 30 hosts finishes a full pass in about an hour. These figures are arithmetic on stated assumptions, not measurements.

**Evaluation** follows GOV.UK's three pillars: automated LLM-as-judge plus custom metrics, expert and red-team review, and live monitoring. The judging criteria are groundedness, relevance, factual accuracy, completeness and reputational safety, with guardrails both before and after generation ([Inside GOV.UK](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2026/05/15/developing-gov-uk-chat-our-data-science-and-ai-engineering-journey/)). For Lietuva Chat that means a gold set of **150–300 LT/EN/RU/UK questions**, each with an expected source URL and key fact, re-run on every index or model change. It also needs a **"canary" subset of volatile facts**, such as the minimum wage (the €1,153 figure used for the 2026 residence-permit means test comes only from a third-party blog ([workinlithuania.com](https://workinlithuania.com/blog/residence-permit-lithuania/)) and is exactly the kind of fact the canary must verify), fees, Sodra rates and deadlines, re-checked whenever the crawler detects a change on the source page. Finally, it needs a Lithuanian-fluency check. GOV.UK's public 90% accuracy target is a reasonable bar to publish against ([RSN](https://rsnonline.org.uk/new-ai-powered-govuk-chat-tool-launched/)).

## Compliance turns on four lines: official texts, TDM opt-outs, impersonation and AI disclosure

**Reuse rights split by content type.** Copyright Law VIII-1185 Art. 5 excludes legal acts, official administrative documents and their **official translations**, registered draft acts, state symbols and "mere data" from copyright ([Wikimedia Commons summary](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Lithuania); [e-seimas VIII-1185](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/portal/legalAct/lt/TAD/TAIS.81676)). TAR content can therefore be indexed and quoted verbatim. Ministry FAQ and explainer pages are a different matter. They may be copyrighted works, and whether they count as "official documents" under Art. 5(2) is a question for a lawyer. A commercial or commercially capable service cannot use the research TDM exception (Art. 22¹). It relies on **Art. 22², the general TDM exception, which rights-holders can opt out of by machine-readable means**. Lithuania transposed it on **1 May 2022** ([Europeana](https://pro.europeana.eu/post/copyright-directive-series-a-closer-look-at-lithuania); [copyrightexceptions.eu](https://www.copyrightexceptions.eu/jurisdictions/lt/)). TDM covers copying for analysis, not showing long passages to the public. So the system should display **short, attributed quotes plus a summary and a link**, and lean on the Art. 24 quotation exception, whose conditions have not been verified. Registers are the riskiest material. Sui generis database rights last 15 years, and the Supreme Court tests infringement by whether a substantial part was extracted and whether that threatens the maker's investment ([LAT](https://www.lat.lt/naujienos/lat-isaiskino-duomenu-baziu-gamintojo-sui-generis-teisiu-apsaugos-salygas/1610)). Use only the open data.gov.lt releases of JAR, and link to the Registrų centras search UIs rather than mirroring them. The reuse law VIII-1524 (as amended by XV-564) is the statutory basis for open data ([e-tar VIII-1524](https://www.e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalAct/TAR.FA13E28615F6/asr)), but its consolidated text could not be retrieved.

**The brand carries an impersonation risk.** The Trademark Law bars marks consisting of the state's official or short name, coat of arms or flag, or imitations of them, without the Minister of Justice's permission ([Infolex VIII-1981](https://www.infolex.lt/teise/DocumentSinglePart.aspx?AktoId=513242&StrNr=1)). "Lietuva Chat" is therefore probably **unregistrable as a trademark**. That limits brand protection but does not, on the evidence found, make use of the name unlawful. The heraldry law prohibits non-compliant or otherwise unlawful use of coats of arms ([e-seimas](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/TAIS.318092/)). Under unfair-commercial-practices law, presenting a service as affiliated with the government is a misleading action once the service monetises in any way ([VVTAT](https://www.vvtat.lt/en/fields-of-activity/unfair-commercial-practices/736)). The non-negotiables follow from this. No Vytis, flag-based logo, Columns of Gediminas or Double Cross. No lrv.lt or epaslaugos.lt look-alike design. No "official" or "valstybinis" wording. A distinct visual identity. And a persistent disclaimer in the footer and the first message stating that the service is independent and not affiliated with the Government of Lithuania or any state institution.

**The AI Act applies now.** Article 50(1) requires that users be told they are interacting with an AI "at the latest at the time of the first interaction" ([artificialintelligenceact.eu Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). The Digital Omnibus left this unchanged, and it has applied since **2 August 2026**. The Omnibus gave Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking a grace period to 2 December 2026 and moved Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)). Lietuva Chat wraps xAI or OpenAI models in its own branded system, so it is arguably a provider for Art. 50(1) and must build the notice into its own product. The high-risk entries most likely to apply, Annex III 5(a) (eligibility for public benefits) and 7 (migration), cover systems used "by or on behalf of" public authorities ([Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)). A private informational assistant is therefore likely outside high-risk. That changes if it partners with Sodra or the Migration Department, or if it starts issuing "you are eligible" determinations. Keep a written Art. 6(3) self-assessment on file. If the service publishes AI-generated public guide pages, Art. 50(4) applies and those pages need an AI-generated label or human editorial review.

**Data protection must be designed in from the start.** Chat logs from migrants and patients will contain special-category data under GDPR Art. 9. Lithuania's ADTAĮ Art. 7 adds national rules: the personal code (asmens kodas) may never be publicly disclosed or used for direct marketing ([Triniti](https://triniti.eu/lt/izvalgos/asmens-kodo-kaip-duomens-teisetas-tvarkymas-pagal-lr-adtai-ir-bendraji-duomenu-apsaugos-reglamenta-ar-kas-nors-keiciasi/)). Whether the amendment aligning its lawful grounds with GDPR Art. 6 is in force was not verified. The minimum design has seven parts. First, detect and redact the 11-digit personal code pattern before anything is logged or sent to the LLM or Exa. Second, warn users in the UI not to enter codes, passport numbers or health details. Third, keep no conversation content by default for anonymous users, or keep it only for a short fixed period such as 30 days. Fourth, offer history only on opt-in, with user-initiated deletion. Fifth, use no chats for training. Sixth, sign Art. 28 processor agreements with xAI, OpenAI, Exa and the database host, with transfer safeguards for the US vendors. Seventh, run a DPIA. VDAI is the supervisor ([VDAI](https://vdai.lrv.lt/uploads/vdai/documents/files/01_%20SolPriPa%20Asmens%20duomenu%20apsaugos%20gaires%20SMULKIAJAM%20IR%20VIDUTINIAM%20VERSLUI%202019-11-08.pdf)). Matching America.gov's no-login, no-retention stance is both good positioning and the cheapest route to compliance.

**Liability for wrong advice cannot be excluded by a disclaimer.** No Lithuanian authority specific to AI advice was found. Civil Code liability and consumer-law review of unfair terms limit how much a disclaimer can do. The defensible pattern is to give general information only, never personalised determinations such as "you qualify" or "you owe €X." Every answer carries a citation and a "last checked" date. The bot refuses or hedges when nothing was retrieved. High-stakes topics (permits, asylum, tax amounts, benefit eligibility, deadlines) get a fixed line routing the user to the Migration Department, VMI, Sodra or International House Vilnius. A "report wrong answer" button feeds a correction log.

## Conclusion

America.gov's launch strengthens Lietuva Chat's case more than it threatens it. It shows that a government-scale "AI front door" built on the same model-plus-retrieval stack ships as a referral engine and is attacked at once for thin answers. An independent service cannot win on authority or transactions, but it can win on **depth per question, languages the state ignores, and answers that cross agency boundaries**, which are exactly where single-agency and single-country state bots stop. The trade-off is structural. Lietuva Chat's credibility has to come from visible provenance (article-level TAR citations and "last checked" dates), because it is legally barred from borrowing credibility from state symbols.

The biggest practical risk is access, not technology. The richest machine-readable source (TAR) is also the safest to use, while the content users need most sits on HTML pages that block bots and whose reuse status is legally grey. That makes an early, written relationship with VSSA and the agencies (asking for crawler access, confirming reuse terms, perhaps offering the eval set back) worth more than any crawler choice. It also positions Lietuva Chat as a complement to a future national assistant rather than its casualty. If the state does build one on its open Lithuanian LLMs, the foreigner, diaspora and cross-border niches remain. A generic Lithuanian-language citizen assistant would not.
